What’s in it

Arc’s sidebar and Spaces, on the engine Safari uses.

Spaces

Tabs down the side, a set for each Space.

Each Space keeps its own tabs and, if you like, its own profile, so work logins never meet personal ones. Swipe between Spaces on the trackpad, or press Control and a number. Favorites sit as icons at the top, pinned tabs return to their page when you close them, and two tabs can share the window in split view. Tabs can go across the top instead, if you’d rather.

The engine

WebKit, in a native app.

Pages are drawn by WebKit, the engine Safari uses. A tab you haven’t looked at for half an hour gives back its memory, then wakes on the same page, at the same scroll, with its history intact. Iris updates itself: a new version downloads in the background and installs when you quit.

Extensions

From the Chrome Web Store.

Chrome extensions run on WebKit’s extension engine, the one Safari’s extensions use. Visit an extension in the Chrome Web Store and Iris shows its own Add button above the page, since Chrome’s won’t work here. Before anything is added, Iris lists what it will be able to do.

Passwords

Kept in your Mac’s keychain.

When you sign in, Iris offers to save the password, and next time it offers to fill it. It fills only on secure pages, and never into a frame hidden inside one. Copying a saved password out of Iris asks for Touch ID first. Passwords exported from Dia or Chrome come in all at once from the file.

Privacy

Ads and trackers blocked.

EasyList and EasyPrivacy, compiled for WebKit and refreshed each week. The shield at the end of the address bar holds everything else for the site: blocking, camera, microphone, zoom and sound. Private windows keep nothing on disk and forget everything when they close. Iris has no accounts and sends no telemetry.

Reading

Reader, Translate, Picture in Picture.

Reader sets an article in EB Garamond, without the page around it. Translate turns a page in another language into yours on your Mac, with Apple’s own translation, and puts the original back with one click. A video can float in Picture in Picture while you read on.

Switching

The first time it opens, it brings your browser with it.

From Arc

Spaces and all.

Every Space with its pinned tabs and favorites, the Spaces’ icons, and your history.

From Dia

Each profile.

Each profile’s open and pinned tabs, and your history. Your passwords follow from the file Dia exports.

From Chrome and Safari

Bookmarks and history.

From Chrome, also your open and pinned tabs. macOS keeps both browsers’ files private, so Iris asks for Full Disk Access first. Nothing in the old browser is changed.
